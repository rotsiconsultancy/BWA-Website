The gown is a size too big. The cap will not sit straight. Somewhere behind you, an aunt is ululating, a cousin is shouting his name, and the photographer at the gate is waving the next family forward.

He is laughing, a little embarrassed by all of it. You are trying not to cry.

Graduation day goes by fast. The flowers wilt by Monday, the cake is gone by evening, and the gown goes back to the hire shop. So what do you give him that lasts longer than the day?

Something he can wear into whatever comes next.

## Why a bracelet

A graduation is a turning point. The years of lectures, deadlines and late nights are over, and something new is about to start: a first job, a new city, a bigger version of who he has been becoming.

A bracelet goes with him. To the first interview, the first day in the office, the first time someone calls him "sir". Every time he notices it on his wrist, he will remember who was cheering at the gate.

The right one depends on who you are to him. So let's start there.

## If you are his girlfriend

You have seen the side of his degree nobody else saw. The 2 a.m. calls before an exam. The group project he carried. The week he nearly gave up and didn't.

Your gift can be personal without being heavy. Something with a bit of fun in it, that suits the man he is now, not the one in the formal photos.

## A few things to get right

**His size.** Borrow a watch he already wears and measure the strap from the buckle to the hole he uses, or loop a string around his wrist while he is distracted. Add 1 to 2 cm, then pick Small (16 cm), Medium (18 cm) or Large (20 cm).

**Your timing.** Order a week or two before the ceremony. That leaves time to confirm delivery with us on WhatsApp, or to collect it instead.

**The note.** Do not skip it. Tuck a card into the box with one line he can keep:

- From a girlfriend: *"I was there for every all-nighter. I'll be there for whatever's next."*
- From a wife: *"You did this while carrying everything else. I'm so proud of you."*
- From a mother: *"You let go of my hand at the classroom door. Look how far you walked."*

And on his wrist, if you look closely, is the bracelet you gave him that morning. Still there in every photo that came after.
