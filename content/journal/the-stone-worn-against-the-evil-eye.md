Somebody admires your new watch a little too long. A colleague congratulates you on the promotion, and the smile doesn't quite reach their eyes.

Most of us would shrug it off. But for thousands of years, across half the world, people have believed that a look like that can carry something with it: envy, bad luck, a quiet curse. They call it the evil eye.

And for just as long, people have been wearing something to look back.

## An old idea, everywhere

The belief in the evil eye is one of the most widespread there is. You find it around the Mediterranean, through the Middle East, across South Asia and down the coast of East Africa. The details change from place to place, but the idea is the same: envy has power, and it travels through a look.

The answer, almost everywhere, was an eye of your own. A charm that stares straight back and turns the bad look away before it lands.

## The stones people chose

Besides the blue glass eye charms you see in markets today, people have long looked to stones that seemed to have eyes in them.

In the first century, the Roman writer Pliny listed stones named for eyes: a "wolf's eye", a "goat's eye" and the "eye of Belus". He was often sceptical about the powers people gave them, but he recorded how many people believed.

In parts of Italy, people wore pieces of green malachite cut into eye shapes and set in silver, to keep the evil eye away. We told more of malachite's story in [Malachite: Africa's Copper Green](/journal/malachite-africas-copper-green).

## The eye that opens

Then there is tiger's eye.

Polish a tiger's eye bead and something strange happens. A band of light appears across it, and as you turn your wrist, the band slides from side to side, like an eye opening and following you. The effect comes from countless fine fibres lined up inside the stone, which catch the light all together. We explained how in [Tiger's Eye: The Stone With Light Inside](/journal/tigers-eye-the-stone-with-light-inside).

No ancient writer names tiger's eye, so we won't pretend one did. Its link to the evil eye is modern. But it is easy to see why it happened. If you were looking for a stone that watches back, you could hardly find a better one. Today, many people wear tiger's eye as a shield against envy, and crystal lovers call it the stone of courage, focus and confidence.

It is also one of Africa's own famous stones. The best-known tiger's eye comes from South Africa's Northern Cape.
