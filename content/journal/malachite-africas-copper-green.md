Put a malachite bead in your palm and two things stand out. It is heavier than it looks. And it is green in a way almost nothing else is: deep forest green, wrapped in rings of lighter and darker bands, like the grain of a tree.

That green comes from copper. Malachite is what copper turns into when water and air get to it slowly, over a very long time. It is one of the oldest stones people have worked with, and today much of it comes from Africa.

## What is malachite?

Malachite is a mineral made of copper carbonate. By weight, more than half of it is copper. Its name comes from an old Greek word for the mallow plant, whose leaves share the same green.

It is a soft stone. On the Mohs scale, which ranks how easily a mineral scratches, malachite sits at about 3.5 to 4. Glass and quartz are both harder. That softness matters when you wear it, and we will come back to it below.

## Why malachite has green bands

Malachite is a secondary mineral. It does not come straight out of the earth's depths like lava. It forms close to the surface, above copper ore, where rainwater seeps down and slowly breaks the ore apart. The copper dissolves, travels a little way in the water, and settles again as malachite.

It does not settle all at once. It builds up in layer after layer, often in rounded, bubbly masses inside cracks and hollows in the rock. Each time the water changes, a little more or less copper, a little faster or slower, the next layer comes out a slightly different green. Cut through that mass and polish it, and the layers show as bands, curves and round "eyes".

## Where malachite comes from: Africa's Copperbelt

Because malachite only forms where there is copper, the best malachite country is copper country. In Central Africa, a band of copper-rich land called the Copperbelt runs across the border of Zambia and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. It holds one of the largest copper reserves in the world.

Much of the malachite sold for jewellery and carving today comes from the Congo side, and Zambia is a source too. The banded Congolese material is the malachite most people picture. In the 1800s, Russia's Ural Mountains were the famous source, but those deposits are now mostly worked out.

## A green sign of copper

Malachite is always tied to copper ore. It forms in the weathered zone just above copper deposits, often next to its blue cousin, azurite. So for anyone searching for copper, green staining on a rock face is a clue worth following.

People followed that clue a very long time ago. Malachite was one of the first ores ever used to make copper. In Egypt, malachite was mined in the Sinai from around 4,000 BC, for copper, for carving and for colour.

## Malachite in ancient Egypt

Ancient Egyptians ground malachite into a fine powder for green eye paint. The powder was crushed on flat stone palettes, and both men and women wore it. Palettes with traces of green malachite on them, and small lumps of the ore, have been found in graves.

Over time, a black eye paint made from galena, a lead ore, became more common. Green malachite stayed in use as a pigment, though. It is one of the oldest green colours known in painting and appears in Egyptian tomb paintings.

## How to recognise genuine malachite

Malachite is one of the easiest stones to recognise once you know it:

- **Look at the bands.** Natural bands wander. They curve, split and form round "eyes", and no two beads carry the same pattern.
- **Feel the weight.** Malachite is a dense stone, so even a small bead feels solid.
- **Feel the temperature.** It starts cool against your skin and warms slowly.
- **Look at the depth.** Malachite is opaque: the green sits on the surface like polished wood grain, not glowing through.

## How to care for a malachite bracelet

Malachite asks for a little more care than most stones. It is soft, it reacts with acids, and long contact with water can dull its polish. None of that means you cannot wear it every day. It just means a few habits:

- **Take it off for water.** Swimming, showering, washing up and training are all times to leave it on the side.
- **Perfume and lotion first, bracelet last.** Let them dry before you put it on.
- **Keep it away from cleaners.** Acidic or household cleaners can mark the surface.
- **Store it on its own.** Harder stones can scratch it, so keep it in a pouch or a separate spot.
- **Clean it gently.** A soft, dry cloth is all it needs.

Malachite also contains copper, so it should not be cut, sanded or ground at home. Beads like ours come finished and polished, ready to wear.

## What malachite is said to mean

Malachite is traditionally associated with protection. In the past, malachite beads were hung on babies' cradles in the belief that they kept the child safe and helped it sleep. In parts of Italy, eye-shaped pieces set in silver were worn against the evil eye. Much earlier, in ancient Rome, malachite was worn as an amulet in the belief that it guarded children.

Those are beliefs, not proven effects. But it is easy to see why the stone collected them. It is a green that grew slowly, layer by layer, out of copper and water. Whatever you wear it for, you are wearing one of the oldest colours people have ever loved.
