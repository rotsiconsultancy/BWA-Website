Most precious stones are small. You wear them in a ring, or on a chain, and they catch the light from a few millimetres across.

Rhodonite was different. In Russia, it was cut in pieces big enough to build with.

## A rose with dark veins

Rhodonite takes its name from *rhodon*, the Greek word for rose. It is a manganese mineral, and at its best it is a deep, warm pink. Through that pink run veins and patches of black, which are manganese oxide.

The contrast is the point. Soft and strong at the same time: pink, with something hard running through it.

## The stone of the Urals

The most famous rhodonite came from the Ural Mountains, near the city of Yekaterinburg. Russia's imperial stone-cutters carved it for palaces and for the imperial family.

Its grandest use was in St Petersburg. In the Peter and Paul Cathedral, where Russia's tsars are buried, the tomb of Empress Maria Alexandrovna is carved from Ural rhodonite. It was made between 1887 and 1906.

A stone the colour of a rose, chosen to honour an empress. It is hard to think of a more romantic history for a stone.

## What rhodonite is said to mean

Today, crystal lovers call rhodonite the stone of compassion, forgiveness and love. You can see why: a gentle pink that holds firm, even with dark lines running through it.

These are beliefs, not proven effects. But it makes rhodonite a lovely gift, a way of saying something warm without having to say it out loud.
